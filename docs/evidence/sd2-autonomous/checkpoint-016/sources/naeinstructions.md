# The NAEHCY Scholars Program

### 2026 Overview

The NAEHCY Scholars Program will award up to ten $3,000 scholarships to postsecondary
students who have experienced homelessness* and are actively pursuing their
post-secondary education. Selected scholars are chosen from a pool of applicants who
meet all eligibility criteria and submit all required materials by the deadline.

Selected scholars are invited, encouraged, and financially supported to attend NAEHCY’s
annual conference (November) where they participate in enrichment and professional
development activities, share their experiences with NAEHCY stakeholders, and participate
in an awards ceremony. NAEHCY Scholars will be invited to participate in two additional
convenings designed to support their educational journey and provide personal and
professional enrichment as well as mentorship opportunities.

Below you will find all of the important information necessary to apply. Please visit our
website at naehcy.org/scholarships/naehcy-scholars-program to learn more about the
program, read the Frequently Asked Questions, and find additional scholarship resources
for students.

### Eligibility

To be eligible for the scholarship, applicants must meet the following criteria:

● Applicants must be under the age of 23, as of July 31, 2026. .

● Applicants must be actively pursuing postsecondary education/trade school.

● Applicants must be eligible for McKinney-Vento assistance or have
experienced homelessness* (while in school) within the last six years.

● Applicants must submit a letter from their McKinney-Vento liaison, verifying
their eligibility. (Liaison should be from their current school district or the
district from which they graduated.)

---

● Applicants may alternately provide eligibility verification from a school official,
outreach worker, shelter provider, religious leader, or other homeless service
provider, confirming current or former homeless status.

● **Please note,** undocumented students who meet the above criteria ARE
eligible. However, **_previous recipients of a NAEHCY scholarship within the
past three years are NOT eligible._**

**The applicant’s homeless status is defined by the federal McKinney-Vento Homeless Education Act, which defines a person
considered to be homeless as one who “lacks a fixed, regular, and adequate night-time residence.” This includes people living
in shelters, cars, motels/hotels, campgrounds, or places not meant for human habitation. The definition includes children
and youth who are living with friends/relatives temporarily (doubled-up) because they lack permanent housing.*

# 2026 Scholarship Application: Additional Materials

### Personal Essay

Please complete a personal essay (500-800 words, double spaced) that
addresses one of the following questions.

1. What would be the one thing you would change about the educational
system for students experiencing homelessness, and why?

2. If you were an entrepreneur, what would you develop, and why?

3. As you look back, what would be one piece of advice you would give to
someone starting High School, and why?

4. Looking forward, what is something you plan to change about yourself, or
add to your life, and why?

5. What is your biggest challenge? If you woke up tomorrow and that
obstacle was gone, how would you go about your day?

6. What activities or interests do you have that support your educational
goals (i.e. church, community service, sports, the arts, etc.)?

### Recommenders and References

● A letter from your local homeless education liaison, school official, outreach
worker, shelter provider, religious leader, or other homeless service provider,
confirming current or former homeless status. This individual must include
their contact information and signature on the letter.

● A minimum of one letter of recommendation from a teacher, counselor, or
other adult (not related to applicant) that can speak to your qualifications and
experiences. This individual must include their contact information and
signature on the letter.

### Tips for Completing Your NAEHCY Scholarship Application and Essay

### ● Be Honest and Reflective

○ Share your personal story authentically. This scholarship honors
resilience, not perfection.

○ Speak from the heart about how your experiences have shaped your
perspective, values, and goals.

### ● Highlight Your Strengths Through Challenges

○ Show how you have navigated obstacles—resilience, responsibility,
adaptability, or leadership in difficult times.

○ Don’t focus only on hardships; emphasize how you respond to
challenges.

### ● Share Your Educational Journey

○ Explain how homelessness or foster care impacted your education.

○ Highlight key academic achievements, even small ones, and moments of
perseverance.

### ● Connect Your Story to Your Future Goals

○ Talk about your dreams for college and career. How has your past shaped
what you want to pursue?

○ Show how receiving this scholarship will help you reach those goals.

### ● Include Moments of Growth

○ Reflect on what you have learned about yourself, others, or the world.

○ Discuss turning points: who helped you? What did you discover about
your own strength?

### ● Don’t Be Afraid to Show Emotion

○ It is okay to show vulnerability; your feelings are part of your journey.

○ Discussing how you have felt helps the reader connect with your story.

### ● Focus on Your Voice

○ Write in your own words, as if you’re telling your story to someone who
truly wants to understand.

○ You don’t need big words; being authentic in your essay is your most
powerful tool.

---

### ● Stay Organized

○ Use a clear structure: introduction, body, and conclusion.

○ Begin with a powerful opening, include key moments in the body, and
end with a hopeful and forward-looking conclusion.

### ● Follow the Prompt and ALL Application Instructions

○ Answer one of the specific questions in the NAEHCY Scholarship
application instructions.

○ Please adhere to the required word count of 500-800 words.

○ Be certain to verify your eligibility as a student who has experienced
homelessness by including a letter from your high school
McKinney-Vento liaison or eligibility verification from a school official,
outreach worker, shelter provider, religious leader, or other homeless
service provider confirming your current or former homeless status.

### ● Ask for Feedback

○ Have a trusted teacher, counselor, or mentor read your draft.

○ They can help catch grammar issues and ensure your message is clear
and compelling.

### ● Proofread Carefully

○ Double-check your application and essay for spelling, grammar, and
clarity.

○ Read your essay out loud to hear how it sounds.
